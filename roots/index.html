Education: Tianjin → Hong Kong → Bristol

The pieces were there before the PhD: random walks in the undergraduate thesis, maths turned computational in Hong Kong, and a habit of speaking up for other students.

Tianjin, Hong Kong, Chengdu, Geneva, Bristol: a maths degree at Tianjin University (TJU) with an oar in hand, an exchange year at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) that turned the maths computational, a first internship, and the 3+1 route (three undergraduate years, the exchange year among them, then the fourth as a Bristol MSc) — the first shortcut, which skipped a year.

Fig. 11 — Five cities joined by a random walk, and the 3+1 jump that skipped a year.

The record

Before the first-passage times there was a maths degree in Tianjin — with an oar in one hand. Then Hong Kong, Chengdu, Geneva and Bristol, joined by one long-range jump: the 3+1 route, which let a bachelor's and a master's overlap and skipped a year.

Tianjin gave the foundations and the habit of service: two years of rowing, a student-rights role, a hundred freshmen guided in, essays on Darboux and Hilbert. Hong Kong made the maths computational, with a first neural-network project. Chengdu brought a first office and a first microphone; Geneva, a simulated UN.

Bristol closed the loop: an MSc with Distinction (the top UK grade) and a dissertation on walkers trapped in boxes, which is where Chapter 01 begins.

Mathematics and Applied Mathematics, Tianjin University

2021–2025 in the School of Mathematics, the final year at Bristol via the 3+1 route. Every walker on this site starts here.

Entered the School of Mathematics at Tianjin University in the 2021 cohort, in an honours mathematics class, reading Mathematics and Applied Mathematics until June 2025 — analysis, advanced algebra, real and complex analysis, mathematical modelling — and qualifying for exemption from the national postgraduate entrance exam. The final stage was completed at the University of Bristol through the 3+1 route. On the plate, Tianjin is the origin: every walker leaves from here.

Head of Student Rights, School of Mathematics

Led the Student Rights department of the Tianjin University School of Mathematics student union from September 2022 to June 2023, taking more than forty student issues on accommodation and facilities to resolution and setting up more than twenty conversations between students and the school's administration. It was the first time representing other students — and the role came back, three years later, in Bristol.

The 3+1 route: the first shortcut

The degree's final stage ran through the Tianjin University–University of Bristol 3+1 route: three years at Tianjin — the third spent on exchange in Hong Kong — then the one-year MSc at Bristol. Selection came in November 2022; by September 2024 the move was made. The bachelor's (to June 2025) and the master's (from September 2024) overlap, which is why the plate draws this as a shortcut: a long-range jump that skips a year.

An exchange year at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST)

Spent the 2023–24 academic year as an exchange student at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, taking partial differential equations, numerical solution of PDEs and applied statistics. It was where the mathematics turned computational — and where a first neural-network research project began.

Physics-informed neural networks for differential equations

A research project at HKUST from November 2023 to May 2024 used physics-informed neural networks in PyTorch to solve partial differential equations — one-dimensional diffusion, a two-dimensional wave equation and Laplace's equation in two and three dimensions — comparing uniform and random sampling of the training points. The 22-page report, ‘Using PINN to solve diffusion, wave and Laplace equations’, is dated 31 May 2024. It is the earliest point on the thread from mathematics to AI.

Moved to Bristol in September 2024 for the MSc in Engineering Mathematics. The modules ran from statistical computing and empirical methods, information theory and coding, and renewable energy to mathematical modelling in biology, medicine and public health, methods of AI, and skills in mathematical research. It finished with a Distinction and a dissertation on first passage — see Chapter 01.

Geneva: a UN field-exchange programme

A winter study programme in Geneva built around the UN Office at Geneva, 8–17 February 2025 — WFUNA's Youth Training Program at the UN, with workshops on humanitarian affairs, multilateralism, diplomacy and the Sustainable Development Goals. In a thirty-person simulated UN conference, represented Australia and led the drafting of a fifteen-minute script for a vaccine-cooperation conference; the group visited WIPO, the ILO, the FAO, UNODA, CERN and the IFRC.

BSc thesis: epidemics in heterogeneous spaces

The Tianjin University undergraduate thesis, completed in May 2025, couples lattice random walks with SIR epidemic dynamics to model disease spread in heterogeneous indoor spaces. A revised English edition (2026) corrects the original, proves bounds on the reproduction number, simulates four indoor scenes exactly (office, supermarket, classroom, metro carriage) and reports a pre-registered test against 27 documented outbreaks, including where the model fails. The random walk was already there before the PhD.
