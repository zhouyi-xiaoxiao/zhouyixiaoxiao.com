Silicon Valley, 2025 · an internship at LiveX AI, a Bay Area AI start-up

Summer 2025 in Silicon Valley: an entrepreneurship programme and a research internship at LiveX AI (4–16 Aug) — where my turn toward AI began.

In summer 2025 the walk went far afield: a few days at an engineering summer camp run by HKUST (the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology), then about three weeks in California.

Between the company visits, the internship brought something rarer: a practical question whose answer could be measured.

4–16 Aug 2025 · Completed

Researcher (Internship) at LiveX AI

LiveX AI, a Bay Area AI start-up, helps consumer companies use AI for customer support, retention and growth. The work ran from a survey of document parsers and a six-dimension quality rubric to a vendor compliance matrix for the engineering team, and ended in a benchmark that checks whether each parser’s output can actually answer questions.

24 Jul – 16 Aug 2025 · Completed

Silicon Valley Entrepreneurship Program

About three weeks in the Bay Area on Tianjin University’s Silicon Valley Entrepreneurship Program — a ‘Stanford & Silicon Valley’ programme run with Founders Space, a San Francisco venture builder, for a cohort of about twenty students. I came straight from the Hong Kong camp, and the weeks filled up with a design-thinking workshop, visits to Silicon Valley companies, a symposium at Stanford and evening founder-and-investor meetups — and, inside the same window, a research internship at LiveX AI.

11–16 Aug 2025 · Shipped

LiveX AI internship: a retrieval-QA benchmark for document parsers

The internship’s study became a benchmark in its final week, once the engineering team asked for something it could run. The question behind it: which parser turns product manuals into text an AI assistant can reliably answer questions from? Each parser’s output (plain-text Markdown) is treated as a knowledge base and judged by downstream usefulness rather than visual fidelity: the text is split into passages, the most relevant ones are found, and a model answers only from those passages — it may say ‘not found’. Every answer is scored first by checking for the exact answer, then by a grader following a written scoring guide. Wrong answers given with confidence are tracked separately as overconfidence. Five parsers, nine documents, a hundred questions per document: 900 questions for every parser.
