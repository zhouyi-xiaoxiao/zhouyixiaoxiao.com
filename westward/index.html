The record

In 2025 the walk went west: first five days at a summer camp run by HKUST (the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology), then about three weeks in California.

Between the company visits, the internship brought something rarer: a practical question whose answer could be measured.

Why it matters

It is where the question behind the later AI work first appeared: judge a tool by whether its output answers real questions, not by how it looks.

Research internship at LiveX AI, a Bay Area AI start-up

4–16 August 2025, Silicon Valley: testing how best to turn product manuals into clean text (Markdown) for an AI knowledge base.

LiveX AI’s AI helps consumer companies with customer support, retention and growth. The work ran from a survey of document parsers and a six-dimension quality rubric to a vendor compliance matrix for the engineering team, and ended in a benchmark that checks whether each parser’s output can actually answer questions.

Silicon Valley Entrepreneurship Program

24 Jul – 16 Aug 2025 Completed

It began on 28 June 2025 with a CV emailed to apply — and a revised version nine minutes later. Then about three weeks in the Bay Area on Tianjin University’s Silicon Valley Entrepreneurship Program — a ‘Stanford & Silicon Valley’ programme run with Founders Space, a San Francisco venture builder, for a cohort of about twenty students. Arriving late from the Hong Kong camp, the weeks filled up with a design-thinking workshop, visits to Silicon Valley companies, a symposium at Stanford and evening founder-and-investor meetups — and, inside the same window, a research internship at LiveX AI.

A retrieval-QA benchmark for document parsers

11–16 Aug 2025 Shipped

The internship’s study became a benchmark in its final week. Each parser’s Markdown is treated as a knowledge base and judged by downstream usefulness rather than visual fidelity: the text is split into passages, the most relevant ones are found, and a model answers only from those passages — it may say ‘not found’. Every answer is scored first by exact matching, then by a grader working from a rubric. Wrong answers given with confidence are tracked separately as overconfidence. Five parsers, nine documents, a hundred questions per document: 900 questions for every parser.
