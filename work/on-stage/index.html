On Stage 展台 · zhǎntái · “exhibition stand”

Brand design, trade shows and talks

A company that sold messaging had to learn to show itself as an AI company — in English, on trade floors and in print — and its own staff had to learn to work with AI agents.

How a messaging company learns to present itself as an AI company: a first overseas brand push, a logo argued from four sketches into one mark, materials for two trade shows, and a 93-minute primer that explained the large-language-model (LLM) industry through SMS.

Fig. 4 — A trade floor as a field of targets, reached one conversation at a time.

The record

A company that sells messaging — Yuexin International, a cloud-communications company — had to learn to show itself as an AI company — in English, on trade floors, in print. This chapter is the design and the stage: a brand push, a logo system, two trade shows, and the trainings that taught colleagues to work with agents.

Before MWC Shanghai, a mobile-industry trade show, came the first overseas brand push and a LinkedIn page set up overnight. In July, a logo for the company's AI governance platform was argued into existence from four sketches. In September, a full print kit for the Wholesale World Congress (WWC), a telecoms trade show in Madrid — every QR code decode-tested.

The stage also meant teaching. A 93-minute primer explained the whole large-language-model industry through the SMS business the team already knew, and hands-on trainings scored colleagues on real work done with agents. The trade floor and the training room turned out to need the same skill: making something complicated feel obvious.

‘Trust Joint 2.0’: a logo system argued into existence

Four AI-sketched directions, audited for silhouette and similarity, rebuilt by hand on a 1000-unit grid: 20 master files, a 14-page guide.

An identity for the company's proposed enterprise-AI governance platform, YueXin AI Nexus (阅信智枢): two independent modules joined by one continuous, controlled central path — standing for identity, permission, budget, evidence and hand-off — in violet and amber, clearly apart from the existing red-blue-green ring. Four directions were explored with AI sketches and then audited for silhouette, legibility at small sizes and similarity to existing marks; the chosen mark was rebuilt by hand as vectors on a 1000-unit grid. The delivery: 20 master SVGs, app icons, print files, a 14-page brand guide and a record of how it was made.

The first overseas brand push

Ahead of MWC Shanghai, the company's first overseas brand push: a brand-communications group; an alignment meeting on the English name, logo, website and positioning; a checklist of materials and support; a plan for the English website and LinkedIn; and a task board of twenty items that colleagues could claim for themselves. The goal was simple — to arrive at an international show looking like one company, in English.

On the stand with the Yuexin International team for all three days of MWC Shanghai 2026 (a mobile-industry trade show), 24–26 June at the Shanghai New International Expo Centre. The weeks before the show went into meeting research from public sources and personalised outreach to exhibitors and partners; the days after, into following up. It was the company's first international show under its new English brand — and the first trade floor of the year.

Bilingual business cards, and a brochure rebuilt as vectors

Designed Chinese and English business cards from the official website palette and the vector logo, generated as editable SVG and PDF by script rather than by hand; then rebuilt the company's bi-fold brochure as clean vectors, with its QR codes regenerated. Hands-on AI agent training for operations teams

Ran the operations team's second round of hands-on agent training as lead trainer and technical judge. Each colleague demonstrated a real task running through an AI agent and was scored on time saved, quality, human review and reusability; the sessions covered browser and computer use, cross-app automation, memory and skills, scheduling and alerts. The cases were written up for reuse, and a case-based ‘AI work assistant’ boot camp was designed for the sales department.

‘From SMS to Intelligent Channels’: a 93-minute primer on AI models

An in-person AI fundamentals session for technical and business staff on 5 August 2026, recorded at 1 hour 33 minutes, that explained the LLM industry through the SMS business: an SMS is billed per 70 characters and a model per token, so the team's instinct for counting characters transfers directly. It covered the value chain, tokens and cost, context and caching, retrieval, fine-tuning, agents, hallucination, private deployment, throughput, API aggregation and gateways, and the six questions customers always ask. The materials — a 24-slide deck, a 60-minute script and a handbook with an 81-term glossary, self-tests and customer-dialogue drills — were finalised the next day.

‘Intelligence. Delivered.’ — print and digital kit for a Madrid trade show

In three design rounds over four days, the trade-show kit for the Wholesale World Congress (WWC, a telecoms trade show in Madrid): an English one-pager headlined ‘Intelligence. Delivered.’; a series of single sheets on the company, international messaging, model access and the ecosystem; then — after the team's prep meeting — three double-sided A4 leaflets in English and Chinese with one QR code per sheet, an English company introduction, a supplier questionnaire and business cards for the delegation. Everything was built through an HTML-to-PDF pipeline, and every QR code was decode-tested before print.

‘People. Connected.’ — a contact site for the delegation

A contact microsite for the Wholesale World Congress delegation, reached from a 19 mm QR code on each business card: a page per person with messaging, LinkedIn, email, website and save-contact links, plus a browser-based card editor so that each person can maintain their own. It went live on the company's connect subdomain and was decode-tested card by card.

Live

An interactive SMS market map

A market map shared internally one morning became a dependency-free web page the same day: five columns of buying logic; six customer segments with their explicit and implicit needs; three use cases — one-time passcodes, transactional notices and permission marketing — each with metrics and typical objections; the flows of supply, money and feedback; and a seven-step path to a deal. It exports PNG and per-segment briefs, prints cleanly, and was revised to V1.1 the same day after three separate AI review passes on content, usability and release safety. A rebuilt version is on this site.
