The record

How Yuexin International — a cloud-communications company whose core business is SMS — learned to present itself as an AI company: an English brand push before MWC Shanghai, a logo taken from four sketches to one mark, materials for two trade shows, and a 93-minute primer on how the large-language-model (LLM) industry works, told through SMS.

Before MWC Shanghai, a mobile-industry trade show, came an English brand push and a LinkedIn page set up overnight; then three days on the stand. In July, a logo for the company’s AI governance platform, taken from four sketches to a final mark. In September, a full print kit for the Wholesale World Congress (WWC), a telecoms trade show in Madrid — every QR code decode-tested.

The stage also meant teaching. A 93-minute primer explained how the large-language-model industry works through the SMS business the team already knew, and hands-on training sessions scored colleagues on real work done with agents. The trade floor and the training room turned out to need the same skill: making something complicated feel obvious.

Why it matters

An AI company is judged by how it presents itself — in English, on trade floors and in print — and by whether its own people can work with AI agents.

‘Trust Joint 2.0’: a logo, from sketches to final mark

Four AI-sketched directions, audited for silhouette and similarity, rebuilt by hand on a 1000-unit grid: 20 master files, a 14-page guide.

An identity for the company’s proposed enterprise-AI governance platform, Yuexin AI Nexus (阅信智枢): two independent modules joined by one continuous, controlled central path — standing for identity, permission, budget, evidence and hand-off — in violet and amber, clearly apart from the existing red-blue-green ring. The audit also tested legibility at small sizes; the delivery added app icons, print files and a record of how the mark was made.

MWC Shanghai 2026, a mobile-industry trade show

On the stand with the Yuexin International team — booth N4.E136 — for all three days of MWC Shanghai 2026 (a mobile-industry trade show), 24–26 June at the Shanghai New International Expo Centre. Before the show: meeting research from public sources, written up as 22 meeting dossiers, and 49 personalised meeting invitations sent through the show’s official app, each naming the booth and the day. After it: app chats, business cards and phone screenshots consolidated into one de-duplicated list of 214 leads, sorted into a follow-up order. It was the company’s first international show under its new English brand — and the first trade floor of the year.

‘From SMS to Intelligent Channels’: a 93-minute primer on AI models

An in-person AI fundamentals session for technical and business staff on 5 August 2026, recorded at 1 hour 33 minutes, that explained how the large-language-model industry works through the SMS business: a Chinese SMS is billed per 70 characters, a model per token (the unit of text a model reads and writes), so the team’s instinct for counting characters transfers directly. It ran from the value chain and the cost of tokens to retrieval, agents, hallucination and private deployment — and the six questions customers always ask. The materials — a 24-slide deck, a 60-minute script and a handbook with an 81-term glossary, self-tests and customer-dialogue drills — were finalised the next day.

‘Intelligence. Delivered.’ — print and digital kit for a Madrid trade show

In three design rounds over four days (5–8 September), the trade-show kit for the Wholesale World Congress (WWC, a telecoms trade show in Madrid): a series of five one-pager types, opened by an English sheet headlined ‘Intelligence. Delivered.’; then — after the team’s prep meeting — three double-sided A4 leaflets in English and Chinese with one QR code per sheet, an English company introduction, a supplier questionnaire and four business cards for the delegation. Everything was built through an HTML-to-PDF pipeline, and every QR code was decode-tested before the print files went out on 8 September.
