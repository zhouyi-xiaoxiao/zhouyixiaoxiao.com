On Stage 展台 · zhǎntái · “exhibition stand”

Brand design, trade shows and talks

A company that sold messaging had to learn to show itself as an AI company — in English, on trade floors and in print — and its own staff had to learn to work with AI agents.

How a messaging company learns to present itself as an AI company: a first overseas brand push, a logo argued from four sketches into one mark, materials for two trade shows, and a 93-minute primer that explained how the large-language-model (LLM) industry works, through SMS.

Fig. 4 — A trade floor as a field of targets, reached one conversation at a time.

The record

Yuexin International, a cloud-communications company that sells messaging, had to learn to show itself as an AI company: in English, on trade floors and in print. This chapter is the design and the stage — a brand push, a logo system, two trade shows, and the training that taught colleagues to work with agents.

Before MWC Shanghai, a mobile-industry trade show, came the first overseas brand push and a LinkedIn page set up overnight; then three days on the stand. In July, a logo for the company's AI governance platform was argued into existence from four sketches. In September, a full print kit for the Wholesale World Congress (WWC), a telecoms trade show in Madrid — every QR code decode-tested.

The stage also meant teaching. A 93-minute primer explained how the large-language-model industry works through the SMS business the team already knew, and hands-on training sessions scored colleagues on real work done with agents. The trade floor and the training room turned out to need the same skill: making something complicated feel obvious.

‘Trust Joint 2.0’: a logo system argued into existence

Four AI-sketched directions, audited for silhouette and similarity, rebuilt by hand on a 1000-unit grid: 20 master files, a 14-page guide.

An identity for the company's proposed enterprise-AI governance platform, YueXin AI Nexus (阅信智枢): two independent modules joined by one continuous, controlled central path — standing for identity, permission, budget, evidence and hand-off — in violet and amber, clearly apart from the existing red-blue-green ring. Four directions were explored with AI sketches and then audited for silhouette, legibility at small sizes and similarity to existing marks; the chosen mark was rebuilt by hand as vectors on a 1000-unit grid. The delivery: 20 master vector files (SVG), app icons, print files, a 14-page brand guide and a record of how it was made.

MWC Shanghai 2026, a mobile-industry trade show

On the stand with the Yuexin International team — booth N4.E136 — for all three days of MWC Shanghai 2026 (a mobile-industry trade show), 24–26 June at the Shanghai New International Expo Centre. Before the show: meeting research from public sources, written up as 22 meeting dossiers, and 49 personalised meeting invitations sent through the show’s official app, each naming the booth and the day. After it: app chats, business cards and phone screenshots consolidated into one de-duplicated list of 214 leads, sorted into a follow-up order. It was the company’s first international show under its new English brand — and the first trade floor of the year.

Hands-on AI agent training for operations teams

Ran the operations team's second round of hands-on agent training as lead trainer and technical judge. Each colleague demonstrated a real task running through an AI agent and was scored on time saved, quality, human review and reusability; the sessions covered browser and computer use, cross-app automation, memory and skills, scheduling and alerts. The cases were written up for reuse, and a case-based ‘AI work assistant’ boot camp was designed for the sales department.

‘From SMS to Intelligent Channels’: a 93-minute primer on AI models

An in-person AI fundamentals session for technical and business staff on 5 August 2026, recorded at 1 hour 33 minutes, that explained how the large-language-model industry works through the SMS business: a Chinese SMS is billed per 70 characters, a model per token (the unit of text a model reads and writes), so the team's instinct for counting characters transfers directly. It covered the value chain, tokens and cost, context and caching, retrieval, fine-tuning, agents, hallucination, private deployment, throughput, API aggregation and gateways, and the six questions customers always ask. The materials — a 24-slide deck, a 60-minute script and a handbook with an 81-term glossary, self-tests and customer-dialogue drills — were finalised the next day.

‘Intelligence. Delivered.’ — print and digital kit for a Madrid trade show

In three design rounds over four days (5–8 September), the trade-show kit for the Wholesale World Congress (WWC, a telecoms trade show in Madrid): a series of five one-pager types, opened by an English sheet headlined ‘Intelligence. Delivered.’; then — after the team’s prep meeting — three double-sided A4 leaflets in English and Chinese with one QR code per sheet, an English company introduction, a supplier questionnaire and four business cards for the delegation. Everything was built through an HTML-to-PDF pipeline, and every QR code was decode-tested before the print files went out on 8 September.

An interactive SMS market map

An interactive, bilingual map of how enterprises buy SMS and where delivery really succeeds. It follows one text message from a business to a phone — who carries it, which way the money and the delivery receipts travel, and what ‘delivered’ can mean — then maps who buys, what they need and how they choose. It began as a whiteboard sketch and became a dependency-free web page the same day, revised after three AI review passes on content, usability and release safety. The version on this site was rebuilt for a general reader.

WWC 2026 in Madrid, a telecoms trade show

With the company’s four-person team at the Wholesale World Congress 2026 in Madrid (16–18 September), the carrier-wholesale industry’s annual meeting, looking for partners in SMS routes, one-time passcodes, RCS (richer mobile messaging) and eSIM (a digital SIM). The company had no stand of its own; the team met partners at theirs. Before the show, meeting requests went to 25 carriers and messaging hubs. On site: a live bilingual schedule of 22 meeting slots, kept in Madrid and Beijing time, and 31 exhibitor dossiers written into the team knowledge base during the show. Then back to Bristol, straight into the new academic year — from trade floor to lecture theatre.
