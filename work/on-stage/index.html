Chapter 04 · Design

On Stage

Brand design, trade shows and talks

Giving a cloud-communications company an AI-era face: a logo system, materials for two trade shows (MWC Shanghai, WWC Madrid), a 93-minute AI primer.

It began with a name. Yuexin International — a cloud-communications company — was heading to MWC Shanghai, a mobile-industry trade show. It needed an English name, a clear position and a LinkedIn page; the page went up overnight. Everything after was made in the same spirit: business cards drawn by a script in the website’s own colours, a personal contact page behind the QR code on each card, and an interactive map of the SMS market.

The stage also meant the training room: hands-on sessions in which every colleague showed a real piece of work done with agents and was scored on it. The trade floor and the training room turned out to need the same skill: making something complicated feel obvious.

23 Jul 2026 · Completed

‘Trust Joint 2.0’: a logo, from sketches to final mark

An identity for the company’s proposed enterprise-AI governance platform, Yuexin AI Nexus (阅信智枢): two independent modules joined by one continuous, controlled central path — standing for identity, permission, budget, evidence and hand-off — in violet and amber, clearly apart from the existing red-blue-green ring. The audit also tested legibility at small sizes; the delivery added app icons, print files and a record of how the mark was made.

5–8 Sep 2026 · Shipped

‘Intelligence. Delivered.’ — print and digital kit for a Madrid trade show

In three design rounds over four days (5–8 September), the trade-show kit for the Wholesale World Congress (WWC, a telecoms trade show in Madrid): a series of five one-pager types, opened by an English sheet headlined ‘Intelligence. Delivered.’; then — after the team’s prep meeting — three double-sided A4 leaflets in English and Chinese with one QR code per sheet, an English company introduction, a supplier questionnaire and four business cards for the delegation. Everything was built through an HTML-to-PDF pipeline, and every QR code was decode-tested before the print files went out on 8 September.

5 Aug 2026 · Delivered

‘From SMS to Intelligent Channels’: a 93-minute primer on AI models

An in-person AI fundamentals session for technical and business staff on 5 August 2026, recorded at 1 hour 33 minutes, that explained how the large-language-model industry works through the SMS business: a Chinese SMS is billed per 70 characters, a model per token (the unit of text a model reads and writes), so the team’s instinct for counting characters transfers directly. It ran from the value chain and the cost of tokens to retrieval (looking things up before answering), agents (AI that does tasks for you), hallucination (confident invention) and private deployment (running on your own servers) — and the six questions customers always ask. The materials — a 24-slide deck, a 60-minute script and a handbook with an 81-term glossary, self-tests and customer-dialogue drills — were finalised the next day.

24–26 Jun 2026 · Attended

MWC Shanghai 2026, a mobile-industry trade show

On the stand with the Yuexin International team — booth N4.E136 — for all three days of MWC Shanghai 2026 (a mobile-industry trade show), 24–26 June at the Shanghai New International Expo Centre. Before the show: meeting research from public sources, written up as 22 meeting dossiers, and 49 personalised meeting invitations sent through the show’s official app, each naming the booth and the day. After it: app chats, business cards and phone screenshots consolidated into one de-duplicated list of 214 leads, sorted into a follow-up order. It was the company’s first international show under its new English brand — and the first trade floor of the year.

16–18 Sep 2026 · Attended

WWC 2026 in Madrid, a telecoms trade show

With the company’s four-person team at the Wholesale World Congress 2026 in Madrid (16–18 September), the carrier-wholesale industry’s annual meeting, looking for partners in SMS routes, one-time passcodes, RCS (richer mobile messaging) and eSIM (a digital SIM). The company had no stand of its own; the team met partners at theirs. Before the show, meeting requests went to 25 carriers and messaging hubs. On site: a live bilingual schedule of 22 meeting slots, kept in Madrid and Beijing time, and 31 exhibitor dossiers written into the team knowledge base during the show. Then back to Bristol, straight into the new academic year — from trade floor to lecture theatre.
