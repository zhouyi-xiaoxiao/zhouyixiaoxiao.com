The record

In June 2026 the research gained a second track: R&D Scientist (AI) at Yuexin International, a cloud-communications company. The work is to build AI that the whole company uses every day — safely, with governance built in, and on infrastructure the company controls.

It began with an internal gateway, built in four days: sign in with Feishu (also known as Lark, a workplace chat-and-docs suite), get a key, see your usage. Then connectors that let agents work inside the company's own workspace as the signed-in person, and an enterprise platform for governed model access that went live in a week, reviewed by swarms of AI auditors before launch.

Then products for people: an AI career-matching platform for a university's Beijing centre, and an assistant for every colleague, built and launched in a day, now becoming a desktop agent that asks before it sends. The rule throughout: humans hold every gate that has consequences.

A Chinese SMS classifier that runs on the device

For a messaging company, an on-device classifier for Chinese SMS: a large language model acted as the teacher, labelling examples, and its judgement was distilled into a lightweight student that sorts each message into verification codes, notifications or marketing in well under a millisecond. Three student models were compared on a labelled test set; a support-vector machine (a classic, lightweight classifier) won, with a macro-F1 of 0.91 (an average score that weights every category equally). Fraud detection was deliberately left out of scope.

A thesis: communication is the execution layer for AI agents

In May 2026, in a series of memos and one-pagers, an argument took shape: communication becomes the execution layer for AI agents. An agent that can reach people through the channels they already use — messages, notifications — is an agent that can finish a job. It is a personal thesis rather than company strategy, and it explains why a first-passage mathematician ended up building AI systems inside a messaging company.

Feishu connectors that let AI agents work inside the workspace

A suite of MCP connectors (MCP is an open protocol that lets AI call outside tools) that lets coding agents work inside the company’s workspace in Feishu (also known as Lark, a workplace chat-and-docs suite) — documents, sheets, chats, meeting minutes and approvals — with scoped permissions, acting as the signed-in person rather than as an all-powerful bot. Written documentation made it something colleagues could install, not just a demo.

An internal AI gateway with single sign-on

Within four days an open-source LLM gateway became the company's internal AI platform, the YueXin Intelligent Open Platform. The gateway was chosen by reading the source code of two candidates rather than their brochures. The work covered a deployment runbook, white-labelling and full Chinese localisation of the admin interface, a mobile layout, and a self-service portal where colleagues sign in with Feishu, get a key and see their own usage. Router retries, fallbacks and per-member budgets followed, and a pre-launch audit of model and permission boundaries closed the week.

A morning briefing written by an agent

A daily agent that turns official public procurement notices into leads: it reads each notice, pulls out what is being bought and what is required, and writes a brief that a person can follow up. Everything with consequences — registering, buying documents, pricing, submitting — stays with people.

22 Jul 2026

Live

An enterprise platform for governed AI access

The company's enterprise AI unified-access and governance platform went from local build to live in a week, launching on 31 July 2026. It is a self-built portal over an open-source gateway: organisations, teams and virtual keys, model allow-lists, budgets and usage, a data-loss-prevention hook and an audit hash-chain, with the gateway's own admin interface closed to the internet. Around launch it went through several rounds of adversarial review by parallel AI agents; privacy-canary checks that planted synthetic identifiers and confirmed none survived in storage or logs; and formal production acceptance, with a receipt for every model.

25–31 Jul 2026

Live

Zijing Career Compass

Zijing Career Compass is an AI career-matching platform for a university's Beijing centre. Students upload a CV, confirm the profile the system builds from it, and see explainable matches against employers on six dimensions, next to a radar of talent policies. The first version was built overnight from meeting notes and went live after a front-end rebuild, followed by a whole-site audit. Later versions added separate workspaces for students, employers and staff, and machine-checked ‘truth gates’ that stop banned wording, personal data and sample placeholders before anything ships. Operations were then handed over to the product owner. The demo runs on synthetic data.

31 Aug – 5 Sep 2026

Live

YueXin AI Assistant — web and Windows

In one day, an assistant for company staff was built and launched on the company's model gateway. Colleagues sign in with Feishu and are provisioned automatically, with a personal monthly quota and self-serve API keys. The web app streams its answers, folds away the model's reasoning, renders Markdown and maths, accepts images and has light and dark themes; a Windows desktop client shipped alongside it, with a download page. Before release it went through three parallel security reviews and an independent review by a second model.

21 Sep 2026

Live

From chat window to desktop agent

The desktop assistant is being rebuilt as a coding and office agent: an open-source agent engine underneath, a YueXin interface on top. Planned for the next rounds: a built-in browser and terminal, scheduled tasks, skills, and acting in Feishu, the company’s workplace suite, on the person’s behalf — with a confirmation before every send, share or approval. It is still in development; these are plans, not shipped features.
