The Engine Room

R&D Scientist (AI) at Yuexin International, a cloud-communications company

Since June 2026 I have built five AI systems, all live: a gateway, a governance platform, Feishu connectors, a briefing agent and a staff assistant.

Why a cloud-communications company? A month before joining, an argument had taken shape in a series of memos: AI agents will reach the real world through messaging, because an agent that can find people where they already are is one that gets things done. Since June the work has tested that from the inside — the plumbing first, then products for people.

Jun 2026 · Ongoing

R&D Scientist (AI), Yuexin International

Joined Yuexin International (brand: Yuexin Cloud) in June 2026, alongside the PhD; the company’s business spans messaging, voice and AI. Besides the systems below, the work has included drafting the company’s AI plan in July and serving as lead trainer and technical judge for the operations team’s agent training.

21 Sep 2026 · Live

Yuexin Cloud AI Assistant — web and desktop

In one day, an assistant for company staff was built and launched on the company’s model gateway. Colleagues sign in with Feishu and are provisioned automatically, with a personal monthly quota and self-serve API keys. The web app streams its answers, folds away the model’s reasoning, renders Markdown and maths, accepts images and has light and dark themes; a Windows desktop client shipped alongside it, with a download page. Before release it went through three parallel security reviews and an independent review by a second model.

10–13 Jun 2026 · Live

An internal AI gateway with Feishu sign-in

Within four days an open-source gateway for large language models (LLMs) became the company’s internal AI platform, the Yuexin Intelligent Open Platform. The gateway was chosen by reading the source code of two candidates rather than their brochures. The work covered a deployment runbook, white-labelling and full Chinese localisation of the admin interface, a mobile layout, and a self-service portal where colleagues sign in with Feishu (also known as Lark, a workplace chat-and-docs suite), get a key and see their own usage. Router retries, fallbacks and per-member budgets followed, and a pre-launch audit of model and permission boundaries closed the week.

25–31 Jul 2026 · Live

A unified AI access and governance platform

The company’s unified AI access and governance platform went from local build to live in a week, launching on 31 July 2026. It is a self-built portal over an open-source gateway: keys issued by organisation and team, a list of allowed models, budgets and usage, a data-loss-prevention check, and an audit log kept as a hash chain, so that tampering with earlier entries can be detected; the gateway’s own admin interface is closed to the internet. Around launch it went through three kinds of check: several rounds of adversarial review by parallel AI agents; privacy ‘canary’ checks that planted synthetic identifiers and confirmed none survived in storage or logs; and formal production acceptance, with a receipt for every model.

1 Jun 2026 · Live

Feishu connectors for AI agents

A suite of MCP connectors (MCP is an open protocol that lets AI call outside tools) that lets coding agents work inside the company’s workspace in Feishu (also known as Lark, a workplace chat-and-docs suite) — documents, sheets, chats, meeting minutes and approvals — with scoped permissions, acting as the signed-in person rather than as an all-powerful bot. Written documentation made it something colleagues could install, not just a demo.

22 Jul 2026 · Live

A morning briefing written by an agent

A daily agent that turns official public procurement notices into leads: it reads each notice, pulls out what is being bought and what is required, and writes a brief that a person can follow up. Everything with consequences — registering, buying documents, pricing, submitting — stays with people.
