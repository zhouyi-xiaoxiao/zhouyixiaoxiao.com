About this site

How this site is made, and what it does not do.

The maths behind the plate

The plate is a toy model: thousands of random walkers on a grid drift toward the event you choose, and with probability β they take one of the shortcuts, jumping a long way at once. The chart shows when they arrive. When both the fast (shortcut) and the slow (long-way) routes carry enough walkers, two peaks appear. The exact model from the paper — a lazy ring with one directed shortcut — is in Chapter 01.

How it was built

Designed and built to one specification, from a verified, privacy-tagged record of the years it describes. Every fact comes from that record; nothing private is published.

Type

Bodoni Moda for display, Newsreader for English text, IBM Plex Mono for data, and Noto Serif SC for Chinese — the large glyphs and the body text alike — all open-licensed and self-hosted.

The name

周易 (Zhōuyì) is also the Book of Changes — a manual for reading chance. 潇潇 (xiāoxiāo) is the sound of rain. Without tones, the name also reads 周一小小 (Zhōuyī xiǎoxiǎo): a tiny Monday.

Say it roughly ‘JOE-ee SHYOW-shyow’ (Zhōuyì Xiāoxiāo).

首达 shǒudá, ‘first arrival’ — typed as toneless pinyin (romanised Chinese), shouda is also 手打, ‘typed by hand’ (Chapter 02).

What kind of figure

Every figure carries a tag — exact, toy model, illustrative or measured — so you always know what kind of picture you are looking at.

What kind of figure: exact
Computed exactly from the model; no sampling noise.
What kind of figure: measured
Real measurements, with their sources.
What kind of figure: illustrative
A drawing of the idea; the numbers are for feel only.
What kind of figure: toy model
A simplified model built to show the idea, not to predict.

Privacy
