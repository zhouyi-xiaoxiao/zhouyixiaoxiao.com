Chapter 07 · Venture

Map in. Traps out.

BioPath · Early Career Enterprise Fellowship

First-passage maths as a product: BioPath tells farms where to put pest traps, with the proof attached. Early Career Enterprise Fellow, 2026.

A pest wandering a farm is a random walker; a trap is the target where its walk ends. That is first-passage maths — and, in 2026, it became a product pitch.

Between the fellowship and the festival came the training: New Enterprise, Bristol’s start-up programme, with weekly workshops and a first pitch award in March 2026; a Growth-stage adviser from the university’s commercialisation team; and two rounds of coaching for a PechaKucha, a talk of 20 slides at 20 seconds each.

23 Dec 2025 · Awarded

Early Career Enterprise Fellow, 2026

The application, on 11 December 2025, proposed turning first-passage research into trap-placement software. On 23 December the University’s enterprise team confirmed the place, and it was accepted the same day.

23 Feb 2026 · Live

The BioPath platform: map in, traps out, proof attached

The software behind the fellowship: a Python platform with a command-line tool, an API and a static pitch site. It takes a site map and a monitoring budget and returns trap or sensor coordinates that optimise first-passage capture, together with a proof bundle anyone can re-run — the optimised score against heuristic and random baselines, a robust worst-case score and the Monte Carlo runs behind them. The automated benchmark loop has made nearly 2,000 commits so far, and the pitch site is live.

A PechaKucha (20 slides, 20 seconds each) at the Festival of Enterprise

Presented BioPath as a PechaKucha (20 slides, 20 seconds each, no going back) at the University of Bristol Festival of Enterprise in the Arts Complex on 10 June 2026, with a printed poster for the showcase and a one-line promise: ‘Map in. Traps out. Proof attached.’ The talk also lives as a web deck, and that same night it became a subtitled pitch video (now on WeChat Channels). For the fellowship showcase three weeks later, a five-minute pre-recorded version followed.
