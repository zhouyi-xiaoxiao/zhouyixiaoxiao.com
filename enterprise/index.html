Map in. Traps out. 地图进，布点出

First-passage maths becomes a product prototype: BioPath takes a site map and a budget, proposes trap locations and supplies a reproducible model-comparison report. The enterprise journey includes an Early Career Enterprise Fellowship, a New Enterprise pitch award and a Festival of Enterprise PechaKucha.

Fig. 7 — How trap placement changes expected capture time in a model field.

Start with these highlights

A random walk can model a pest moving through a site; traps become absorbing targets. Under those assumptions, trap placement changes the expected time to capture. In 2026 this became the BioPath product pitch: map in, trap locations out, with a reproducible comparison report.

The Early Career Enterprise Fellowship came first, in December 2025; then a New Enterprise pitch award, a growth cohort, training in teams and funding, and a platform that optimises trap placement within its model. Its report compares the proposed layout with heuristic and random baselines; that is model evidence, not a guarantee of field performance.

The story was then told in public three times: a twenty-slide PechaKucha at the Festival of Enterprise, a feature on the university's enterprise blog, and a Careers Service spotlight that begins in a strawberry patch.

Early Career Enterprise Fellow, 2026

An application on 11 December 2025 proposed BioPath, ‘a bio-inspired optimisation tool for precision pest management’ that turns first-passage research into trap-placement software. On 23 December the university's enterprise leadership sent a successful outcome, and the fellowship was accepted the same day — one of twelve Early Career Enterprise Fellows in the 2026 cohort.

The BioPath platform: map in, traps out, comparisons attached

The software behind the fellowship: a Python platform with a command-line tool, an API and a static pitch site. It takes a site map and a monitoring budget and proposes trap or sensor coordinates under a first-passage model. The reproducible report compares the optimised score with heuristic and random baselines, includes a robust worst-case score and records the Monte Carlo runs behind them. These are model comparisons, not a guarantee of field performance. An automated benchmark loop has pushed nearly two thousand benchmark commits on its own, and the pitch site is live.

For the fellowship showcase three weeks later, a five-minute pre-recorded version followed.
