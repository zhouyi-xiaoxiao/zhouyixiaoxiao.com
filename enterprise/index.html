The record

A pest wandering a farm is a random walker; a trap is the target where its walk ends. Where you put the traps decides how soon the pest is caught. That is first-passage maths, and in 2026 it became a product pitch: BioPath — map in, traps out, proof attached.

The Early Career Enterprise Fellowship came first, in December 2025; then a New Enterprise pitch award, a growth cohort, training in teams and funding, and a platform that returns optimal trap coordinates with a proof bundle anyone can re-run.

The story was then told in public: a twenty-slide PechaKucha at the Festival of Enterprise, a feature on the university’s enterprise blog, a Careers Service spotlight, ‘From strawberry patch to PhD’ — and, on WeChat Channels, the pitch video and an interview.

Why it matters

Where a trap goes decides how soon a pest is caught — and better placement can cut the reliance on chemical rodenticides.

The BioPath platform: map in, traps out, proof attached

The software behind the fellowship: a Python platform with a command-line tool, an API and a static pitch site. It takes a site map and a monitoring budget and returns trap or sensor coordinates that optimise first-passage capture, together with a proof bundle anyone can re-run — the optimised score against heuristic and random baselines, a robust worst-case score and the Monte Carlo runs behind them. An automated benchmark loop has pushed nearly two thousand benchmark commits on its own, and the pitch site is live.
