The record

Some of the record keeps itself: nearly four thousand videos on a phone since 2023, a LinkedIn headline frozen in 2024, a first personal website. This chapter is the signal — what was published, what was kept, and how it is being turned into something others can watch.

In August 2026 a WeChat studio began turning the archive into short videos, with a pipeline that has already found the speech in over a thousand clips — and a rule that a person always presses publish. A home-built bilingual subtitle engine carries the pitch videos. Only aggregates appear here; the clips stay private.

And a making-of: a first prototype of this site in August, a blunt critique a month later — not enough light, achievements hard to find — and the plate you saw on the front page as the answer.

Light painting, 2023

In May 2023 a portfolio of light-painting photographs came together: long exposures in which a moving light draws paths that accumulate on the sensor over several seconds. It is the direct ancestor of this site's central image — a dark plate on which the paths of thousands of walkers accumulate into light.

Food philosophy, PhD edition

A LinkedIn post set out a ‘food philosophy, PhD edition’ as an optimisation problem: stay reliably fed without leaving the office too often. Two objectives — speed (under fifteen minutes a meal, often under ten, because other work runs in parallel while a potato is in the microwave) and cost — and one hard constraint: nutrition must be complete, macro and micro. It is a joke, and it is also exactly how everything else on this site gets decided.

A home-built bilingual subtitle pipeline

The local build of ffmpeg shipped without a subtitle renderer, so subtitles took another route: every bilingual cue is rendered as a transparent image and overlaid, in chunks of about ninety seconds processed in parallel — roughly twenty times faster, with an 8 min 44 s 4K clip rendered in about two and a half minutes. A native Swift tool followed in July, and caption rules in August: at most two lines, 18 Chinese or 46 English characters per line, zero overlaps, and a terminology audit.

‘First-Passage Universe’: the first prototype of this site

The first design for this site, in August 2026, was a canvas of eight artboards: a particle-field hero, a prologue, a memory galaxy, a two-trajectory timeline, a digital twin and a research page. Reopened a month later, it earned a blunt verdict — no feeling of light and gradient, and no way to see the achievements — and a sharper brief followed: disruptive, playful, deeply artistic, strongly interactive, and a real summary of everything so far. The luminous plate on the front page, with the achievements beside it, is the answer.

Finding the speech in 3,947 videos

A resumable background pipeline exported each video, extracted its audio, deleted the intermediate file and ran voice-activity detection, all under an adaptive disk budget on a machine short of space. Over about thirty-six hours it processed 3,943 of the 3,947 videos with no outstanding failures, and flagged 1,052 as containing speech. Only these counts are published; the speech itself includes other people's voices and stays private.

A WeChat content studio

In August 2026 an AI-assisted studio was set up to turn years of phone footage into short videos for WeChat Channels (视频号) and a WeChat Official Account (公众号). Four goals frame it: map every video in the archive; build a voice and a digital presence with clear AI disclosure; profile the speaking style and suggest how to improve it; and semi-automate publishing — with the final ‘publish’ click always made by a person. The transcription route is chosen and the pipeline is built; the topic map, and a ‘memory galaxy’ for this site, come next.

This site: First Passage, Nº 01

The site you are reading, in its first edition: a live first-passage experiment in which walkers leave Tianjin and search a luminous plate of 2021 to now for whatever you choose to measure. It replaces the page that, since April 2026, simply forwarded zhouyixiaoxiao.com to LinkedIn. It was designed and built with a large team of AI agents working to one specification, with every fact drawn from a verified, privacy-tagged record — and nothing private shipped.
