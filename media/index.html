The record

Some of the record keeps itself: nearly four thousand videos on a phone since 2023, a LinkedIn headline frozen in 2024, a first personal website. This chapter is the signal — what was published, what was kept, and how it is being turned into something others can watch.

In August 2026 a WeChat studio began turning the archive into short videos, with a pipeline that has already found the speech in over a thousand clips — and a rule that a person always presses publish. A home-built bilingual subtitle engine carries the pitch videos. Two of them are out on WeChat Channels under the name 周易潇潇 (Zhouyi Xiaoxiao): the Festival of Enterprise pitch (June 2026) and an interview, ‘Taking research into the real world’ (August 2026) — both told in Chapter 07. By 30 September 2026 the channel held 262 videos and 1,856 followers. Of the camera roll, only aggregates appear here; its clips stay private.

Studio

A WeChat content studio

Launched August 2026: an agent-run studio turning years of phone footage into short videos for WeChat — a human always presses publish.

In August 2026 an AI-assisted studio was set up to turn years of phone footage into short videos for WeChat Channels (视频号, WeChat's short-video feed) and a WeChat Official Account (公众号, its long-form channel). Four goals frame it: map every video in the archive; build a voice and a digital presence with clear AI disclosure; profile the speaking style and suggest how to improve it; and semi-automate publishing — with the final ‘publish’ click always made by a person. The transcription route is chosen and the pipeline is built: a resumable pass over the whole library found speech in 1,052 of the 3,947 videos, and only such counts are ever published. The channel's name is 周易潇潇.

Launched

A home-built bilingual subtitle pipeline

The local build of ffmpeg shipped without a subtitle renderer, so subtitles took another route: every bilingual cue is rendered as a transparent image and overlaid, in chunks of about ninety seconds processed in parallel — roughly twenty times faster, with an 8 min 44 s 4K clip rendered in about two and a half minutes. A native Swift tool followed in July, and caption rules in August: at most two lines, 18 Chinese or 46 English characters per line, zero overlaps, and a terminology audit.

‘First-Passage Universe’: the first prototype of this site

The first design for this site, in August 2026, was a canvas of eight artboards: a particle-field hero, a prologue, a memory galaxy, a two-trajectory timeline, a digital twin and a research page. Reopened a month later, it earned a blunt verdict — no feeling of light and gradient, and no way to see the achievements — and a sharper brief followed: disruptive, playful, deeply artistic, strongly interactive, and a real summary of everything so far. The luminous plate on the front page, with the achievements beside it, is the answer.
