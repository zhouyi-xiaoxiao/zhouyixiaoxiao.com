Skipping the IME? Keyboard-Friendly Multilingual Prompting for LLM Agents

Xiaoxiao Zhouyi (byline form of Zhouyi Xiaoxiao), Yanzhen Li

UKAIRS 2026 (UK AI Research Symposium) · Emerging Research track · poster · Edinburgh · Accepted 11 Aug 2026 · poster #181, 25 Nov 2026

In a 48-call pilot: yes for routine prompts — but homophones need key words in characters.

  • Poster list (external link)

The record

It started as a personal itch, and it was answered the slow way: real coding agents, paired prompts, an independent audit before every spend, and the failures kept.

It ran in phases, from late May to early June 2026, with a person authorising every real call. The calls went to two coding agents, Codex and Claude Code, and the second reproduced the homophone split. The paper went to UKAIRS in June and was accepted in August. An eight-language follow-up is built but not yet run.

Why it matters

Many languages are typed through an input method — software that turns typed letters into characters — or a romanisation: pinyin (romanised Chinese), Japanese romaji, Arabizi. Knowing where meaning survives the shortcut tells people when they can skip that step with an AI agent.

The key finding: toneless pinyin erases homophones

事实 and 实施 are both ‘shi shi’. Pinyin-only prompts scored 0.333; keeping the key word in Chinese characters restored 1.00.

Three adversarial tasks hinge on words that collapse without tones: shu alone is 书 ‘book’, 树 ‘tree’, 输 ‘lose’ and 熟 ‘ripe’. Native characters scored 1.00, and so did the mixed workflow — instructions in pinyin, critical terms in characters. Clean pinyin, unspaced pinyin and pinyin with typos all averaged 0.333: the agent left the answer in pinyin or picked the wrong character. The one task that survived, 买 ‘buy’ against 卖 ‘sell’, was saved by 入 ‘in’ and 出 ‘out’. The mixed prompt still needs an estimated 0.815 of the native keystrokes; typing effort itself was not measured.

ime-eval: a harness for keyboard-friendly prompting

28 May 2026 Completed

ime-eval is the instrument behind the study. It drives real command-line coding agents rather than raw APIs, pairs every native-script prompt with keyboard-friendly variants — clean pinyin, no spaces, light typos, initials, and mixed prompts that keep key terms in native script — and defines profiles for eight input styles, from Japanese romaji and Vietnamese keyboard input to Hinglish, Arabizi, Russian transliteration, Greeklish and Korean. Hard rules from day one: configurations generated automatically, dry-run by default, results written once, and sandboxes that keep answers from leaking to the agent. Named here, not yet public.

A negative result: restoring the Chinese first made it worse

31 May 2026 Completed

A small twelve-call repetition reproduced the lower score for abbreviations: direct answers to pinyin-initial prompts averaged 0.444 against 1.0 for native prompts. A ‘normalise, then answer’ wrapper did not rescue it. The mean fell to 0.333 because the model judged the initials too lossy and declined to answer in all three repetitions — turning confident wrong output into honest abstention. Native prompts were unaffected. It is a negative result, and it stayed in the paper.

UK AI Research Symposium (UKAIRS 2026), Edinburgh

24–25 Nov 2026 Scheduled · poster on 25 Nov

UKAIRS 2026, the UK AI Research Symposium, runs on 24–25 November 2026 at the John McIntyre Conference Centre in Edinburgh; poster #181 is up on Wednesday 25 November, 13:15–14:15. The plan, written into the paper, is a side-by-side demo — the native prompt, its keyboard-friendly variant, the agent’s response, the grade and the effort estimate — so that visitors can toggle from routine prompts to homophone traps and watch where meaning collapses.
