Skipping the IME?

First AI paper · UKAIRS 2026, the UK AI Research Symposium

Can agents follow pinyin (romanised Chinese) alone? Accepted as a poster at UKAIRS 2026 in Edinburgh (first author, with Yanzhen Li). In 48 exploratory runs, everyday instructions worked; proper names and homophones needed characters.

It started as a personal itch, and it was answered the slow way: real coding agents, paired prompts, an independent audit before every spend, and the failures kept.

It ran in phases, from late May to early June 2026, with a person authorising every real call. The calls went to two coding agents, Codex and Claude Code, and the second reproduced the homophone split. The paper went to UKAIRS in June and was accepted in August. An eight-language follow-up is built but not yet run.

31 May 2026 · Completed

The key finding: toneless pinyin erases homophones

Three adversarial tasks hinge on words that collapse without tones: shu alone is 书 ‘book’, 树 ‘tree’, 输 ‘lose’ and 熟 ‘ripe’. In this exploratory test, native characters scored 1.00, and so did the mixed workflow — instructions in pinyin, critical terms in characters. Clean, unspaced and misspelt pinyin all averaged 0.333: the agent left the answer in pinyin or picked the wrong character. The one task that survived, 买 ‘buy’ against 卖 ‘sell’, was saved by 入 ‘in’ and 出 ‘out’. An uncalibrated estimate puts the mixed prompt at 0.82 of the input-method keystrokes; typing effort was not measured.

11 Aug 2026 · Accepted

UKAIRS 2026: first-author paper accepted as a poster

On 11 August 2026 UKAIRS accepted the paper for a poster session in the Emerging Research track. The reviews found the question genuinely interesting, the tool potentially useful and the inclusivity angle worth pursuing, and recommended leading with the ‘shi shi’ example and the three-layer analysis — encoding change, compression, tone loss. It is my first peer-reviewed AI paper, and I am first author.

NeurIPS 2026 Fusion Equilibrium Challenge: second place in Challenge 1 and an invited talk

The Fusion Equilibrium Challenge, in the NeurIPS 2026 Competition Track, asks for the magnetic flux map of a tokamak (a fusion device) to be predicted from its coil currents and Thomson-scattering profiles (laser measurements of the plasma), without magnetic sensors. In the blind Final, with results announced on 14 November 2026, the solo entry took second place in Challenge 1, on DIII-D, with a score of 0.997253, and 13th place in Challenge 2, zero-shot transfer to MAST. The invited 15-minute talk at the competition workshop in Atlanta on 12 December 2026 is an interactive web page rather than slides. The code is public under the MIT licence.

25 Feb 2026 · Ongoing

An AI-native way of doing mathematics

Across the first-passage line a repeatable loop took shape: AI models propose derivations and code, separate agents attack the claims, numerical computation acts as the arbiter, and exact algebra is machine-checked in Lean 4, a proof assistant. The documented cases include proposed formulas that failed numerical checks and were discarded, a repository-grounded audit that caught errors an outside model could not see, and multi-round reviews — up to about sixty agents in a single audit — before each submission. The principle in one line: AI can generate more scientific possibilities; the research skill is deciding which ones deserve belief.
