The record

The account dates from December 2021, but the building is recent: almost all of it lands in 2026 — two hackathons in London, a route page for a snowy Iceland drive, two guides for Bristol law students.

And the small fixes in between: a browser that went deaf, seven dead ends before a working keyboard shortcut, an always-on agent host that taught its lessons and was then honestly uninstalled.

Why it matters

Small public tools are where new methods get tried first, at a scale where failing is cheap.

London Cuts

An AI-native travel-story and postcard studio in three visual modes — Fashion, Punk, Cinema — with a live public demo.

A creator tool for a single-place trip: photos placed by their own metadata, written stories and AI-generated postcards in six styles, with public reader pages, a map atlas and PDF or PNG export. The live demo tells ‘A Year Around London’ in thirteen stops and ‘A Week in Reykjavík’, and it has entry points for AI agents too: an llms.txt, an OpenAPI description and an MCP endpoint (MCP is an open protocol that lets AI call outside tools). London Cuts is a working name.

The Tianjin University maths guide

22 Jul 2023 – 18 Dec 2025 Live

Instead of only using the curriculum, write a guide to it for everyone who comes after. Built from July 2023, the Tianjin University maths undergraduate guide gathers what a student needs — a foreword, the professional courses, the majors and how to contribute — first as a quick guide with a document library, then as a MkDocs site. It has been served at zhouyixiaoxiao.info since December 2025 and is the oldest thing on this page, still online.

SocialOS: 1,222 commits in five days

5–9 Mar 2026 Completed

SocialOS turns raw notes, voice memos, screenshots and encounters into structured memory about people and events, ‘self-mirror’ reflections and drafts for each platform, delivered through a publishing queue that a human approves. It runs only on the local machine. It was built for the UK AI Agent Hack in five days, with 1,222 commits between 5 and 9 March 2026 — 1,084 of them carrying an ‘[autodev]’ prefix from the automated agent factory orchestrated for the week. The public repository keeps the pitch pack and the evidence.

CatchMouse, rebuilt for Apple Silicon

29–30 May 2026 Shipped

A clean Swift rewrite of CatchMouse, an Intel-only utility from 2011: a menu-bar agent that warps the cursor to the left, centre or right display with global hotkeys, cycles between displays, remembers custom hotkeys per monitor, flashes display numbers and offers command-line verbs. It builds a universal app with only the command-line tools and needs no accessibility permission. MIT-licensed.
